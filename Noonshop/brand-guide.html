The Founding Story

THE FIT
that was missing.

Patients weren't just struggling to see. They were struggling with the glasses themselves. Frames slipped down noses. Temples pinched. Lenses sat improperly aligned, subtly distorting the very vision they were meant to correct. For Dr. Anna Park, MD, these weren't isolated incidents. Over more than two decades in ophthalmology, they were a pattern.

Red marks and discomfort were common, but often dismissed as unavoidable. To Dr. Park, they pointed to something systemic: most frames were designed around a narrow set of facial assumptions, leaving millions with eyewear that corrected their vision but compromised their comfort and daily quality of life.

"Consumers can easily find lenses that match their prescription, but far fewer can find frames that truly fit their face."

Dr. Anna Park, MD · Founder

The realization became personal when she searched for properly fitting glasses for her son. Three optical shops. No frames that actually fit. "That's when it became clear," she recalls. "This wasn't just my son's issue. It was everywhere." Noon Shop was her response: frames engineered for low bridges, high cheekbones, wider and narrower face shapes: the full range of structure the industry had quietly decided to ignore.

Medical-grade titanium and stainless steel throughout, chosen for how they wear, not how they photograph. Meaningful innovation, it turns out, often lies in addressing problems that have been normalized. In eyewear, that problem is fit.

Model & Image Direction

THE FACE
that wears it.

Our visual direction is an extension of the brand's core promise. Faces of the world, faces of all kinds, and the full range of structures that come with them. Beautiful, but never a single archetype of beautiful. The price point demands polish. The mission demands range. Both must show up in the same image.

Facial Structures to Represent
LOW BRIDGE

East Asian, Southeast Asian, Black, and Latinx facial structures. Flatter nasal bridges where standard frames slip forward. The face this brand was explicitly built to serve.

HIGH CHEEKBONES

Frames that press on or ride over prominent cheekbones are a persistent, ignored discomfort. Show faces where the frame clears correctly, visibly settled, not fighting the face.

WIDER & NARROWER

Both ends of the face-width spectrum, underserved by standard sizing. Temples that sit flush, without squeeze or gap. The frame disappears into the face because it belongs there.

Shot Types
CLOSE
PORTRAIT

Chest to crown. Direct eye contact or 15° turn. Shows how the frame sits on the bridge and against the cheekbones. Primary commercial shot. This is the one that sells.

THREE-
QUARTER

45° angle. Reveals the temple arm and how the frame wraps the ear. Best for showing fit quality and frame depth. The shot that builds trust.

PROFILE

Pure side view. Shows frame depth, temple angle, and lens position against the cheekbone. The fit shot. Nothing hides here.

Light & Background
  • →Soft directional natural light or controlled studio. Even, not dramatic.
  • →Background: cream (#F6F4EF) or paper (#E1DDD3). On-brand, never distracting.
  • →No harsh shadows. Light should reveal the facial structure, not flatten or obscure it.
  • →Skin tones rendered accurately and warmly across all ethnicities. No whitening or homogenizing.
Expression & Styling
  • →Calm and composed. No broad smiles or extreme editorial expressions. Serene, assured.
  • →Clothing neutral or out of frame. The face and frame are the only subjects.
  • →Natural makeup. Present but not dominant. The face at its best, not its most transformed.
  • →Ages 25–50. The premium buyer — not exclusively young, not uniformly aspirational. West Coast tech skews 28–42.
Audience Context · West Coast Tech

A significant segment of the Noon Shop buyer is the West Coast technology professional — designers, engineers, founders, and product thinkers. This audience is educated, detail-oriented, and comfortable spending $300–400 on something that genuinely performs. They are drawn to function-forward design, clinical credibility, and material specificity. They are not moved by overt branding or fashion-led imagery.

Model imagery for this audience should feel considered and intelligent, not aspirational in the traditional luxury sense. Real faces. Quiet confidence. A person who found the right frame, not a person performing wearing one. The Bay Area and Pacific Northwest are also among the most ethnically diverse regions in the country, which reinforces the brand's range-of-faces direction naturally.

Do
  • Cast a genuine range of ethnicities and facial structures
  • Show the frame fitting correctly — settled, not slipping or pinching
  • Use calm, composed expressions that convey quiet confidence
  • Keep backgrounds on-brand: cream or paper, no lifestyle staging
  • Vary angles across portrait, three-quarter, and profile within a set
  • Keep styling minimal — the face and frame are the only subject
Don't
  • Cast exclusively high-fashion or one type of face
  • Use lifestyle backgrounds, props, or active/movement shots
  • Generate over-smoothed, AI-homogenized skin — preserve texture
  • Use dramatic lighting that hides how the frame actually sits
  • Apply heavy makeup or styling that competes with the frame
  • Show frames sitting off-center, tilted, or appearing to slip

Diverse by design. Every face is a face Noon Shop was built for.

How We Scale

AI SYSTEM
Brand Intelligence.

THIS DOCUMENT IS THE BRIEF.

The Noon Shop Brand Guide is the authoritative source of truth for all AI-assisted content generation across every channel and format. Every output, written, visual, or interactive, is measured against what is defined here.

This document functions as the system prompt for Noon Shop's AI content infrastructure. It defines the rules the system operates within. Not suggestions. Rules.

What the System Governs
PRODUCT COPY

Product descriptions, frame specifications, collection introductions, and fit language. Every word written about a Noon Shop frame begins with fit as the primary value. Materials are named with specificity, titanium, stainless steel, never described by trend or season alone.

STUDIO MODEL PHOTOGRAPHY

AI-generated model imagery for commercial, editorial, and social use. The locked scene spec defined in the Photography section is the only accepted input. Background, light source, color temperature, expression, clothing, and crop are fixed. The face and frame are the only variables.

PRODUCT PHOTOGRAPHY

AI-generated product detail and flat lay imagery. Frames on cream or paper. Material fills the frame. No props, no lifestyle staging. The frame tones connect directly to the brand accent. The frame is the story.

EMAIL MARKETING

Campaign and transactional email copy. Brand voice applies in full: direct, confident, human, refined. Subject lines lead with fit or a specific clinical truth. No breathless superlatives, no exclamation marks, no fashion-first language.

WEB DESIGN & LANDING PAGES

Page layouts, section hierarchy, CTA copy, and interaction patterns follow the motion and typography principles defined here. Bebas Neue for all display. Playfair Display italic for accent. Outfit 300 for body. Forest is a deliberate accent, never decorative.

GOOGLE & META ADS

Paid search and social ad copy generated from the approved voice pillars: Direct, Confident, Human, Refined. Headlines lead with the fit problem or the fit solution. No generic eyewear language. No fashion trend references. The brand does not compete on price or style. It competes on precision.

INSTAGRAM & SOCIAL CONTENT

Grid tiles, story cards, and caption copy generated to the locked visual system. Three content types only: model portrait, product flat, brand statement tile. All three share the same background, light, and typographic treatment. Consistency is not optional. It is the brand signal.

MARKETING GRAPHICS

Campaign banners, event materials, and branded assets. Color palette, typography, and spacing rules are non-negotiable. Forest enters only where precision meets craft. Never as decoration.

How the System Uses This Document

Before generating any output, the AI references this document to confirm:

  • →Is the primary value fit, not style?
  • →Does the visual follow the locked scene spec?
  • →Is the typography Bebas Neue, Playfair Display italic, or Outfit 300?
  • →Is Forest used as a deliberate accent, not a decoration?
  • →Does the model casting reflect the full range of facial structures this brand was built to serve?
  • →Does the copy lead with clinical credibility, not fashion aspiration?
  • →Is the brand voice Direct, Confident, Human, Refined, in that order?

If any output deviates from these checks, it does not represent the brand.
