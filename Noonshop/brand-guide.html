The Founding Story

THE FIT
that was missing.

Patients weren't just struggling to see. They were struggling with the glasses themselves. Frames slipped down noses. Temples pinched. Lenses sat improperly aligned, subtly distorting the very vision they were meant to correct. For Anna Park, MD, these weren't isolated incidents. Over more than two decades in ophthalmology, they were a pattern.

Red marks and discomfort were common, but often dismissed as unavoidable. To Anna Park, they pointed to something systemic: most frames were designed around a narrow set of facial assumptions, leaving millions with eyewear that corrected their vision but compromised their comfort and daily quality of life.

"Consumers can easily find lenses that match their prescription, but far fewer can find frames that truly fit their face."

Anna Park, MD · Founder

The realization became personal when she searched for properly fitting glasses for her son. Three optical shops. No frames that actually fit. "That's when it became clear," she recalls. "This wasn't just my son's issue. It was everywhere." Noon Shop was her response: frames engineered for low bridges, high cheekbones, wider and narrower face shapes: the full range of structure the industry had quietly decided to ignore.

Medical-grade titanium and stainless steel throughout, chosen for how they wear, not how they photograph. Meaningful innovation, it turns out, often lies in addressing problems that have been normalized. In eyewear, that problem is fit.

The Collections

THE
two collections.

Noon Shop carries two distinct collections, each engineered around a different philosophy of precision. Both begin with fit, and both refuse to compromise on material. Every style is named for a city, and the city always sits in its collection's part of the world: Dormann for the Americas, Steel Brown for West Europe.

Vibrant colors, shapes, and sizes, because fit and personality are not a trade-off. Dormann is the frame you reach for every day and enjoy every time.

Photography

IMAGE
DIRECTION.

Photography leads with the face and the fit, not the logo. Two libraries carry the brand: warm editorial lifestyle, real people in cafés, galleries, and golden-hour light, and clean studio catalog portraits on a soft cream ground. In both, the frame sits settled and still, and the face is always the subject.

Editorial & Lifestyle

Editorial is warm and lived-in: golden-hour and window light, cafés, terraces, and galleries, champagne and light metal frames. Warm and expressive: real smiles, a little laughter, an easy head tilt. The setting supports the face, it never competes with it.

Catalog & Studio Portraits

Catalog is shot straight on, chest to crown, on a soft cream ground in even light. One frame, one face, filename to SKU. This is the primary commercial shot, the one that shows exactly how a frame sits.

"In an industry where style has long dominated the conversation, Noon Shop is reframing the narrative by bringing function, inclusivity, and medical precision back into focus."

Model & Image Direction

THE FACE
that wears it.

Our visual direction is an extension of the brand's core promise. Faces of the world, faces of all kinds, and the full range of structures that come with them. Beautiful, but never a single archetype of beautiful. The price point demands polish. The mission demands range. Both must show up in the same image.

Facial Structures to Represent
LOW BRIDGE

East Asian, Southeast Asian, Black, and Latinx facial structures. Flatter nasal bridges where standard frames slip forward. The face this brand was explicitly built to serve.

HIGH CHEEKBONES

Frames that press on or ride over prominent cheekbones are a persistent, ignored discomfort. Show faces where the frame clears correctly, visibly settled, not fighting the face.

WIDER & NARROWER

Both ends of the face-width spectrum, underserved by standard sizing. Temples that sit flush, without squeeze or gap. The frame disappears into the face because it belongs there.

Shot Types
CLOSE
PORTRAIT

Chest to crown. Direct eye contact or 15° turn. Shows how the frame sits on the bridge and against the cheekbones. Primary commercial shot. This is the one that sells.

THREE-
QUARTER

45° angle. Reveals the temple arm and how the frame wraps the ear. Best for showing fit quality and frame depth. The shot that builds trust.

PROFILE

Pure side view. Shows frame depth, temple angle, and lens position against the cheekbone. The fit shot. Nothing hides here.

Light & Background
  • →Soft directional natural light or controlled studio. Even, not dramatic.
  • →Background: cream (#F6F4EF) or paper (#E1DDD3). On-brand, never distracting.
  • →No harsh shadows. Light should reveal the facial structure, not flatten or obscure it.
  • →Skin tones rendered accurately and warmly across all ethnicities. No whitening or homogenizing.
Expression & Styling
  • →Warm and genuine. Real smiles, soft laughter, an easy head tilt. Personality is welcome, as long as the fit stays readable.
  • →Clothing neutral or out of frame. The face and frame are the only subjects.
  • →Natural makeup. Present but not dominant. The face at its best, not its most transformed.
  • →Ages 25 to 50. The premium buyer, not exclusively young, not uniformly aspirational.
Audience Context · West Coast Tech

A significant segment of the Noon Shop buyer is the West Coast technology professional, designers, engineers, founders, and product thinkers. This audience is educated, detail-oriented, and comfortable spending more on something that genuinely performs. They are drawn to function-forward design, clinical credibility, and material specificity. They are not moved by overt branding or fashion-led imagery.

Model imagery for this audience should feel considered and intelligent, not aspirational in the traditional luxury sense. Real faces, real warmth. Someone who found the right frame and is glad they did, caught mid-smile or mid-thought, not a model performing. The Bay Area and Pacific Northwest are also among the most ethnically diverse regions in the country, which reinforces the brand's range-of-faces direction naturally.

Do
  • Cast a genuine range of ethnicities and facial structures
  • Show the frame fitting correctly, settled, not slipping or pinching
  • Use warm, genuine expressions: natural smiles, head tilts, easy confidence
  • Keep backgrounds on-brand: cream or paper, no lifestyle staging
  • Vary angles across portrait, three-quarter, and profile within a set
  • Keep styling minimal, the face and frame are the only subject
Don't
  • Cast exclusively high-fashion or one type of face
  • Use lifestyle backgrounds, props, or active/movement shots
  • Generate over-smoothed, AI-homogenized skin, preserve texture
  • Use dramatic lighting that hides how the frame actually sits
  • Apply heavy makeup or styling that competes with the frame
  • Show frames sitting off-center, tilted, or appearing to slip

Diverse by design. Every face is a face Noon Shop was built for.

Brand Voice

HOW WE
SPEAK.

DIRECT

We don't hide behind lifestyle imagery or trend language. Fit is either right or it isn't. We say so plainly, without softening the point.

"Fit is not optional. It never was."

CONFIDENT

We believe fit is fundamental. Not a bonus feature, not a premium upgrade. The brand speaks with quiet clinical assurance, never loudness.

"You weren't hard to fit. The industry just wasn't trying."

HUMAN

Behind the precision is warmth. Anna Park built this for real faces, real people, the ones the industry had been quietly excluding for too long.

"Three optical shops. No frames that actually fit. That's when it became clear: this wasn't just one person's issue. It was everywhere."

REFINED

Grounded in medicine. Expressed in craft. Titanium and stainless steel chosen for how they wear, not how they photograph. Language that ages well.

"Medical-grade materials. Quiet precision. The frame that belongs on your face."

Do
  • Lead with fit as the primary value, always
  • Reference the full range of facial structures we serve
  • Draw on Anna Park's clinical credibility as the brand foundation
  • Name materials with specificity: titanium, stainless steel
  • Use Bebas Neue for all display type
  • Use Navy as a deliberate accent: tags, borders, CTAs
Don't
  • Say "for everyone" (we're specific, not generic)
  • Describe frames by trend or season alone
  • Use exclamation marks or breathless superlatives
  • Reference "fashion" as the primary motivation
  • Add color accents beyond the core palette
  • Place the logo on busy or patterned backgrounds
Motion & Interaction

HOW WE
