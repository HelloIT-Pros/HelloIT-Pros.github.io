The Three Pillars

WHAT FITTING
ACTUALLY
MEANS

Standard eyewear sizing was built around a narrow set of facial geometries. Low bridge. High cheekbones. Wider or narrower face. These are not edge cases — they describe the majority of wearers globally. Noon Shop frames are engineered around three measurable fit dimensions. Each frame in our catalog is rated on all three.
01
⌇
Bridge Fit

LOW BRIDGE
ANATOMY

The bridge sits at the top of your nose. A low bridge means the nose doesn't protrude as far from the face — standard frames slide down because the bridge sits too high and lacks nose pad grip. Low-bridge frames feature a lower nose pad position, wider nose pad angle, and sometimes a keyhole or saddle bridge to distribute weight without relying on protrusion.

"The single most common complaint I hear is frames sliding down by midday. In 90% of cases, it's a bridge fit issue — not the temple, not the weight."
— Dr. Anna Park, MD
02
◠
Cheekbone Clearance

HIGH CHEEKBONE
CLEARANCE

High cheekbones push the bottom rim of frames upward when you smile or speak — causing fogging, smearing, and constant micro-adjustment. Noon Shop frames with high-cheekbone clearance have a reduced vertical lens height or a lifted frame silhouette that maintains gap between the lower rim and cheek surface.

"If your lenses fog every time you exhale — or your frames smear with blush — you almost certainly need more cheekbone clearance."
— Dr. Anna Park, MD
03
↔
Face Width

FACE WIDTH
ALIGNMENT

Frame width should match your face width — measured temple-to-temple at the widest point. When frames are too narrow, temple pressure builds throughout the day. Too wide, and they slide laterally. Total frame width = lens width × 2 + bridge. A well-fitting frame sits symmetrically, with temples just touching (not pressing) above the ears.

"People often size up in frame width to solve bridge problems. That just trades one discomfort for another. Solve the bridge first."
— Dr. Anna Park, MD
How to Measure

FRAME NUMBERS

1

Lens Width (Eye Size)

The horizontal width of a single lens, measured at its widest point. Most Noon Shop lenses range 48–56mm. This number alone doesn't tell you total frame width.

Example: 52□
2

Bridge Width

The gap between the two lenses, measured at the narrowest point across the nose. Lower-bridge frames typically use 14–18mm. This is the most critical measurement for bridge fit.

Example: □16
3

Temple Length

The arm that runs from hinge to ear tip. Most adults fit in 135–150mm. Longer temples accommodate wider heads; shorter ones reduce side pressure.

Example: □□ 144
4

Lens Height

Vertical lens depth. Taller lenses give more visual field; shallower lenses improve cheekbone clearance. Rimless or semi-rimless frames can be custom-cut for your exact height.

Example: □□ 36mm tall
5

Total Frame Width

The full span of the frame. Calculate as: (lens width × 2) + bridge. Should match your face width, measured ear-to-ear at the temples, ±3mm.

52 + 52 + 16 = 120mm total

Face Profiles

YOUR FACE IS
NOT ONE SIZE

LOW BRIDGE
Flat nasal bridge, common in East & Southeast Asian, Latin, and Black facial structures
Flat Bridge Nose Pad Critical Frames Slide

The nose bridge doesn't protrude enough to hold a standard frame in position. Frames slide forward and downward throughout the day. Look for frames with low-set silicone nose pads, a keyhole bridge, or a saddle-style bridge. Bridge width: 14–16mm. Nose pad angle: 20–30°.

Shop Low-Bridge Frames
HIGH CHEEKBONES
Prominent zygomatic arch that interferes with lower frame rim
High Zygomatic Lens Fogging Lower Rim Lift

High cheekbones cause the lower rim to make contact with the cheek during facial movement — smiling, speaking, looking down. This leads to smearing, fogging, and constant repositioning. Shallow lens height (30–34mm) and an elevated frame silhouette solve this without sacrificing optics.

Shop Cheekbone-Clear Frames
WIDER FACE
Temporal face width above 138mm, common across many ethnicities
Wide Temporal Temple Pressure Wider Frame

When frame width is too narrow, the temples press inward against the skull — causing headaches and leave marks above the ears. Total frame width should match or slightly exceed face width. Look for frames with total width of 126–134mm and flexible hinges.

From the Founder

"Fit is clinical. A frame that slides is not a style problem — it is a prescription delivery problem."

Dr. Anna Park, MD · Ophthalmologist · Founder, Noon Shop

After more than two decades treating patients whose visual outcomes were compromised by ill-fitting frames, Dr. Park founded Noon Shop with a single clinical principle: the frame is part of the prescription. Fit is not optional, and it is not one size.

Every Noon Shop frame ships with a fit card specifying which facial structures it was designed for — bridge height, cheekbone clearance, face width. Not as a marketing claim. As a clinical specification.
