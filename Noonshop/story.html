The Beginning

"I watched patient after patient leave my clinic with a perfect prescription — and a frame that negated half of it by sliding off their nose."

Dr. Anna Park, MD · Founder

The pattern was impossible to ignore. Patients with East Asian, Latin, and Black facial structures — a significant share of any ophthalmology practice in any major American city — were consistently underserved by the eyewear available to them. Not because the optics were wrong. Because the fit was.

Standard frames are engineered around a narrow range of facial geometries. Low bridge, high cheekbones, wider face widths — these aren't edge cases. For most of the world, they are the norm. Yet the industry had built its entire sizing system around the exception.

Dr. Park started sketching frame specifications on prescription pads between patients. What began as a clinical complaint became an engineering problem. And an engineering problem, it turned out, had a solution.

Noon Shop launched with a single principle: the frame is part of the prescription. Not an accessory. Not an afterthought. A medical instrument that either works — or doesn't.

The Journey

HOW WE
GOT HERE

Early Career
THE CLINICAL OBSERVATION

During residency and the early years of her ophthalmology practice, Dr. Park begins tracking a pattern: patients are returning for adjustments not because the prescription changed, but because the frame doesn't fit. The complaint is always the same — the frame slides. The cause is almost always the same too: bridge geometry.

Research Phase
TWO YEARS OF MEASUREMENTS

Dr. Park begins measuring facial geometry across her patient base — bridge height, zygomatic arch position, temporal width. She cross-references against the frame specifications her patients are wearing. The mismatch is systematic and consistent. She begins working with an industrial designer to translate her clinical data into frame specifications.

Prototyping
THE FIRST FRAMES

First prototypes are tested with volunteer patients in the clinic. The feedback is immediate and consistent: frames that don't slide, lenses that don't fog, temples that don't press. What felt like an engineering challenge turns out to be a solved problem — one the industry simply hadn't chosen to solve.

Dormann Launch
COLLECTION ONE

The Dormann collection launches — named for the engineering principle behind its snap hinge. Beta-titanium temples, TR90 fronts, South Korean manufacturing. Every specification tied to a fit rationale. Bridge widths from 14–16mm. The frame that finally fits the face that was never designed for.

Steel Brown Launch
COLLECTION TWO

In collaboration with designer Wolfgang Proksch, the Steel Brown collection brings German stainless steel construction and Mazzucchelli Italian acetate to the same fit-first brief. Where Dormann is technical, Steel Brown is architectural. Both share the same clinical backbone.

Now
NOON SHOP TODAY

Every frame in the Noon Shop catalog ships with a fit card. Every product page carries a clinical note from Dr. Park. Every collection is rated on three fit dimensions — bridge, cheekbone clearance, face width. Fit is not a filter. It is the foundation.

DR.
ANNA
PARK
MD · Ophthalmologist · Founder, Noon Shop

Dr. Anna Park completed her ophthalmology residency and subspecialty training in anterior segment disease before joining a private practice in Los Angeles, where she built a patient base that reflected the city's demographic diversity.

That diversity made the problem impossible to ignore. A majority of her patients had facial structures — low bridge, high cheekbones, wider face widths — that standard eyewear wasn't designed for. Adjustments were temporary. The underlying fit never changed.

Dr. Park began approaching the problem as a clinician: measure, document, hypothesize, test. After two years of facial measurement data and six months of prototype iterations, she had a frame specification that actually worked. Noon Shop was the company built to bring it to market.

She continues to practice ophthalmology. Every Noon Shop product page carries a clinical note written by Dr. Park — not as branding, but because she believes the ophthalmologist's perspective belongs at the point of purchase.

How We Build

THE
CRAFT
BEHIND
THE FIT

01
Clinical Specification First

Every frame starts as a clinical specification, not a sketch. Bridge width, nose pad angle, lens height, temple tension — all derived from Dr. Park's patient measurement data before a single design decision is made.

02
Material for Function

Beta-titanium temples because they flex without fatiguing. TR90 fronts because they're lightweight without being fragile. German stainless because it holds tolerance. Materials are chosen for what they do, not what they cost.

03
Manufacturing Partnerships

Dormann is manufactured in South Korea with a facility that specializes in titanium eyewear tolerances. Steel Brown is produced in Germany in collaboration with Wolfgang Proksch's atelier. Both relationships are long-term and exclusive.

04
The Fit Card

Every frame ships with a fit card: bridge specification, cheekbone clearance rating, face width range, and a clinical note from Dr. Park. Because the person who buys the frame deserves to know exactly what it was designed for.

"We did not build Noon Shop to disrupt eyewear. We built it because a frame that doesn't fit is a prescription that doesn't work — and that is a clinical problem that deserved a clinical answer."

Dr. Anna Park, MD · Noon Shop
