02 · The Fit Narrative

Why this brand exists.

Every post answers an unspoken question: will this fit my face? The content only works if this narrative is felt — not explained.

The problem

"Born from the frustration of frames that never quite fit."

Standard eyewear is engineered around European facial geometry — higher nose bridges, narrower cheekbones. For faces with lower nose bridges or wider mid-face proportions, frames slip, gap, and press. The fit was never the person's fault.

The answer

"You were never hard to fit. You were wearing the wrong brand."

Dormann, Steel Brown, and DeNova are engineered for lower nose bridges, higher cheekbones, and a wider mid-face — without sacrificing aesthetics. Beta-titanium temples, W-end pieces, and precise bridge geometry ensure the frame holds exactly where it belongs.

How this reads in content
Influencers

Kai, Yuna, and Mei wear the frames correctly — nose pads flush, temples tucked, no slide. The fit is the visual proof. Never show a frame sliding down or sitting off-centre.

Captions

Captions reference fit implicitly — material, engineering, sizing — not style alone. "14 grams" says more than "beautiful." "54-19-150" is a fit statement. Let the spec carry the weight.

Product shots

Macro hinge details, nose pad close-ups, temple flexibility — these are fit proof points. Schedule at least one macro per two weeks that shows the engineering, not just the aesthetic.

03 · Content Rules

Do. Don't. No exceptions.

Pulled directly from the brand guide, adapted for social. These are not suggestions.

Do
  • →Bebas Neue for all display type on graphics — headlines, collection names, labels
  • →Pair every Bebas headline with a Playfair Display italic subtitle on quote cards
  • →Default to dark backgrounds (#0C0C0C) — light (cream) is the exception, not the base
  • →Gold as a deliberate accent only — rules, tags, CTAs, one typographic highlight
  • →Product photography that shows material and metal — close-up beats lifestyle for trust
  • →Captions: one declarative line, one supporting line — then stop
  • →Write alt text for every image — describe objectively, do not use it as extra caption
Don't
  • ×Exclamation marks — anywhere, ever. Not in captions, not in Stories text, not in alt text
  • ×Colors outside the palette — no blues, reds, greens, or warm skin tones on graphics
  • ×Overuse gold — it loses value when it's everywhere. One gold element per graphic
  • ×Script, decorative, or serif typefaces on graphics — only Bebas, Playfair italic, or Outfit
  • ×Hashtag walls — 3 branded hashtags maximum, or none. Never topic-tag for reach
  • ×Props or lifestyle clutter in product shots — one frame, one surface, nothing else
  • ×Emoji — not in captions, not in Stories, not in bio. The brand does not perform warmth

Product shot rule

Frames on pure white (#F7F6F3) or cream paper. Material fills 60–80% of frame. No props, no lifestyle context. Gold-tone metal directly echoes the brand accent. When held in hand — gold jewellery only, no coloured nails.

Macro rule

Hinge, bridge, or temple tip only. Shot on dark slate or cream paper. 100mm macro equivalent. The gold of the hinge should read as the same gold as the brand accent (#C8B89A). No fingerprints.

On-face rule

Frame must be sharp and centred on the face — not sliding down. Nose pads visible. Temple tucked behind ear. The fit narrative is everything: a slipping frame contradicts the brand's core claim.

13 · Before it ships

The brand check.

If any of these fails, the post doesn't go. No exceptions.

VoiceReads as Direct, Confident, Human, Refined. No hype, no emoji clusters, no exclamation marks.
PaletteCream, black, gold. No warm skin tones, no saturated backgrounds, no competing colors.
Frame in focusFor any shot featuring a model: the frame is sharp and well-aligned on the face.
No AI tellsHands, ears, logos, text, and background signage all read as real.
Pillar taggedThe post is tagged to exactly one pillar and one collection in the asset archive.
Caption sourcedHeadline pulled from the brand guide, not freestyle. Alt text written for accessibility.
