01 · The Origin

A doctor's
diagnosis.

Dr. Anna Park didn't start an eyewear brand. She solved a medical problem that optical shops had ignored for decades.

The Moment

"Three optical shops. Three failed fittings. One child who couldn't see clearly because no frame was built for his face."

As an ophthalmologist, Dr. Anna Park understood vision correction with clinical precision. But when she took her son to be fitted for glasses, the system failed him — not once, but three times. Shop after shop, the response was the same: the frames just don't fit faces like his.

For an MD who had spent years treating patients with similar low-bridge, wider-set facial geometry — predominantly of East Asian descent — this wasn't an inconvenience. It was a diagnosis. The problem wasn't her son's face. The problem was an industry that had never bothered to engineer for it.

The Insight

"Fit is not an aesthetic preference. It is a functional requirement. A frame that slides cannot correct vision."

Standard eyewear is calibrated around European facial geometry — higher nose bridges, narrower cheekbones, more angular profiles. For the estimated 1.5 billion people with low-bridge anatomy, this means frames that gap, slide, fog, and press — compromising both comfort and optical performance.

Noon Shop was founded on a medical insight: a frame that doesn't fit is a frame that doesn't work. Every collection — Dormann, Steel Brown, and DeNova — was engineered backward from that principle. Nose bridge geometry first. Temple flex second. Material weight third. Aesthetics last, but never compromised.

"

Fit is fundamental. It is the single variable that determines whether eyewear functions as a medical device or merely a fashion accessory. We chose to build for function first — and discovered that precision engineering is, by nature, beautiful.

Dr. Anna Park MD — Founder & Chief Designer, Noon Shop

DN
About the Collection

DeNova solves an engineering problem that most eyewear brands don't acknowledge: the hinge. Every frame flexes. Most hinges are a single material — which means they either resist flex (stiff, uncomfortable) or allow too much (frames that lose their shape). DeNova bridges both with a patented dual-material hinge that joins German medical stainless steel to beta-titanium in a single integrated mechanism.

The stainless steel frame body provides the structural rigidity and corrosion resistance required for daily wear. The beta-titanium temples provide memory flex — press them flat and they return to their original geometry within seconds. The hinge translates between the two without stress fractures, fatigue, or play. At 50 grams or under, DeNova is the most technically ambitious frame in the Noon Shop line.

05 · Fit Architecture

The engineering
behind the feel.

Three contact points determine whether eyewear fits. Noon Shop engineers all three with clinical precision — and nothing else.

01
NOSE BRIDGE

Standard frame bridges are set at 22–24 mm for European anatomy. Noon Shop bridges sit at 20–21 mm — matching the narrower nasal geometry common in East Asian, Southeast Asian, and many Latin American facial structures. A correctly-set bridge means the frame sits flat, not angled; flush, not floating.

02
TEMPLE FLEX

Beta-titanium is a shape-memory alloy — it returns to its programmed geometry after flexing up to 40° without fatigue or deformation. Noon Shop temples are pre-set for a wider cranial profile (cheekbone-to-cheekbone), meaning they hold without squeezing. No headache. No pressure marks. Just the frame sitting where it belongs.

03
FRAME WEIGHT

A 30-gram frame exerts ~6g of downward force on the nose bridge per hour of wear. Noon Shop frames, at 14–20g, reduce that load by 40–50%. On a face with a lower nose bridge — where the contact area is smaller — weight is not a comfort issue. It is a medical one. TR90 and stainless steel were chosen for their weight-to-rigidity ratios, not their marketing appeal.

Every specification in our line — lens width, bridge measurement, temple length, material choice — is a fit decision before it is a style decision. The aesthetics follow the engineering. They always have.
