Next Steps for the Gateway Team

What we need from you to make the pilot a success

For: Mike Severe, Lester Severe, pilot users From: HelloIT Pros Updated: 2026-05-23
The system is built, tuned, and acceptance-tested. The remaining gap between "ready for pilot" and "delivering real value" is your team using it during actual work and telling us what's missing or wrong. This page lists everything we need from Gateway on your side. None of it is heavy lift — most items take five minutes.

1 Confirm pilot access This week

Five pilot accounts are active. Each pilot user should do the following once:

  • Open ai.gwaffordable.com in a desktop browser (Chrome, Edge, Safari, or Firefox). Mobile works but desktop is better for the source-citation feature.
  • Log in with your @gwaffordable.com email and the temporary password HelloIT Pros sent you. If you don't have it, email helloit@gwaffordable.com.
  • Change your password from Settings → Account. Use a unique password, not one you reuse elsewhere.
  • Confirm that Gateway Assistant appears in the model picker at the top of a new chat. It should be the only option you see. If you see other models or nothing at all, tell us.
  • Ask one test question to confirm everything works — see the User Guide for ideas. A good first one: "What is the policy on an employee being arrested for DUI?"

If anything in step 1–5 doesn't work, email helloit@gwaffordable.com with what you tried, what you expected, and what you got. Screenshots help. We're actively watching the inbox during the pilot.

2 Use it in real work First 2 weeks

The biggest risk to a pilot is that everyone tests it for ten minutes on day one, then forgets about it. Test-mode usage misses the patterns that matter — the specific questions site managers ask at 3 p.m. on a Tuesday when something is on fire. Real work surfaces the gaps.

What "real use" looks like

  • Next time you'd normally email Mike or text a regional manager asking about a policy, try asking Gateway AI first. If the answer is right, you saved 20 minutes. If it's wrong, you've found a bug — tell us.
  • Next time you'd dig through the Employee Handbook for one paragraph, ask the AI to quote it.
  • Next time you need to pull a clause from a vendor contract (snow removal scope, elevator callback SLA, pest control coverage), let the AI find the section.
  • Next time you need to know who manages a specific property or what type of building it is, just ask.

See the User Guide for the full list of example questions, grouped by topic.

3 Tell us when the AI is wrong Ongoing

The fastest way for the system to get better is for you to flag bad answers immediately. We need three pieces of information:

  1. The question you asked, copied exactly.
  2. The AI's response (the full text, or a screenshot).
  3. What you expected instead — the right answer, the source document the AI missed, or a description of what would have been helpful.

How to send it to us

MethodBest for
Email helloit@gwaffordable.com Anything with a screenshot or longer description. Default channel.
Reply to the weekly check-in email Patterns you've noticed across the week, not a single incident.
Standing call (if scheduled) Discussion of bigger changes — should we add a new collection? Should access be split?

Don't wait until you have a list of ten things. Send each one when it happens. Single-incident reports let us see context that a week-old list of bullet points doesn't.

4 Submit new documents As needed

The AI only knows what we've ingested. When you have new content the team should be able to ask about — a new vendor contract, an updated lease template, a new internal procedure document, an RFP — send it to us and we'll add it.

What's worth sending

  • New or updated vendor contracts (snow, landscaping, pest, elevator, fire monitoring, laundry, etc.)
  • New or revised HR / personnel policies
  • State or program-specific lease templates we don't have yet
  • Updated property roster information (when the Community Listing changes)
  • RFPs you're evaluating
  • Procedure documents (e.g., the P&P Manual when Mike finishes it)

What NOT to send

  • Tenant-specific records (rent ledgers, eviction history, SSNs) — these stay in your property management system. The AI is not designed for tenant lookups.
  • Personal HR records about a specific employee (disciplinary files, salary, medical info).
  • Anything you're not sure about — ask us first.

See the Sharing Documents Guide for the full submission process — preferred file formats, naming conventions, and the Google Drive folder we monitor.

5 Decisions Gateway leadership owns First month

A handful of decisions are yours to make. We'll bring you the data; you make the call.

  • Broader rollout timing. The pilot is 5 users. When are we ready to open it to all Gateway staff, and on what schedule?
  • Who joins the pilot next? Are there specific site managers, regional managers, or accounting team members who should be added before full rollout? Two or three additions during the pilot is the right size.
  • Whether to add per-role assistants. Today everyone uses one "Gateway Assistant" that searches the entire knowledge base. We could add dedicated assistants (e.g., "HR Assistant", "Contract Reviewer") with their own focus. Worth doing if pilot users tell us they'd prefer scoped views.
  • How to handle sensitive data if it ever needs to land in the system. The current corpus is policies + contracts + property catalog — no tenant-personal or employee-personal data. If you ever want to add something sensitive (compliance records, HR personnel files), we'll pre-walk the access-control implications with you and only proceed after your explicit sign-off.
  • P&P Manual completion. Mike has been working on the Policy & Procedures Manual. When sections are done, send them in for indexing. The AI will be noticeably more helpful once they land.
