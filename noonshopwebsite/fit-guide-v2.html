What Fitting Actually Means

Three dimensions.
One perfect fit.

Dimension 01
THE BRIDGE
Where most frames fail.

A bridge that's too wide slides down the nose, shifts the optical centre away from your pupil, and quietly weakens the prescription you paid for. Low and flat bridges are where standard frames fail most — and where the industry has done the least.

How Noon Shop fixes it
Adjustable, low-bridge-optimized nose support that grips gently and keeps the optics aligned to your eyes.
Dimension 02
THE CHEEKBONE
Settled and still.

Frames that rest on prominent cheekbones shift with every expression, smudge the lens, and cause constant unconscious adjustment. Rim height is an engineering choice — and Noon Shop makes it deliberately.

How Noon Shop fixes it
Rim height engineered for clearance, so the frame floats above the cheekbone and stays put through every smile.
Dimension 03
THE FACE WIDTH
No squeeze, no gap.

Temple length determines whether a frame stays put or creates pressure points. Too wide and it falls forward; too narrow and it grips behind the ears. Noon Shop proportions temple length to your actual face width — not a one-size approximation.

How Noon Shop fixes it
Temple length and flex tuned to your face width, so lightweight frames hold securely without ever pinching.
How to Measure

Know your fit
in three steps.

01
YOUR BRIDGE
Look straight in a mirror. If glasses tend to slide down, your bridge is low or flat. If they sit high and steady, it's higher.
02
YOUR WIDTH
Note how frames sit at the temples. Tight behind the ears means a wider face; loose and forward-sliding means narrower.
03
YOUR CHEEKBONES
Smile in the mirror. If frames lift or touch your cheeks, you have prominent cheekbones that need clearance.
